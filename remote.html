Connect to your TV

Tap Find my TV — the page scans your Wi-Fi and locates the TV on its own, even if its IP changed, and detects whether it's LG, Samsung or Roku. Your phone must be on the same Wi-Fi as the TV, and the TV must be on.

Or enter the IP manually (on the TV: Settings → Network → Wi-Fi Connection → Advanced → IP address):

Certificate

The TV uses a self-signed certificate, and the browser decides whether to trust it — a web page can't switch that off. What this page can do is validate it: the check below tries the TV's secure handshake. If it completes, the certificate is trusted and Connect will work. If not, accept the certificate once in Safari and validate again.

First time: the TV should show a pairing prompt — accept it with your normal remote. If that fails, use PIN pairing.

The page connects to the TV directly (no extra software needed). If the direct route ever fails it falls back to a local relay — for that, run python3 relay.py in a-Shell, which also serves this page over your Wi-Fi.

Playback

Voice control

Try: “volume up”, “mute”, “play”, “home”, “open YouTube”, “search YouTube for…”, “turn off”.

Tap once and it keeps listening for command after command — tap again, or say “stop listening”, to switch it off. The page must stay open; it can't listen in the background. Needs microphone permission and internet (the phone does the speech-to-text).

Apps

🔍 YouTube search

Opens YouTube, goes to Search, types your query and runs it.

It navigates with the D-pad (menu → Search), so if it lands on the wrong item, fix it with the D-pad and type in the Keyboard card instead. Samsung: the search screen opens, but type with the TV's on-screen keyboard — Samsung gives remotes no text-input API.

😴 Sleep timer

TV turns off when the timer ends. The page must stay open.

Tip: add this page to your home screen for an app-like remote. The TV must be on — power-on isn't possible over the network.
